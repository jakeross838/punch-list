## The company's page (6 November 2026)

- The page: `apps/company-page`, one small web page (about 33 KB, the script inside it), built by `node tools/build-company-page.mjs` into `apps/company-page/dist`. It is on Expo's hosting for the Punch List project: `https://punch-list.expo.app`, with each link at `https://punch-list.expo.app/c#<token>`. The token goes after the #, so the hosting never sees it. `https://punch-list.expo.app/c#sample` is the sample page, answered in the browser and sent nowhere; the preview's links all go there.
- To put a new version up, from `apps/mobile` with the Expo token from `local/secrets/expo.env` in the environment:

  ```
  node tools/build-company-page.mjs
  cd apps/mobile && npx eas-cli@latest deploy --export-dir ../company-page/dist --non-interactive --prod
  ```

- The function: `supabase/functions/company-page`, deployed with verify_jwt off (the link is the only key). Every call is a POST with the token in its body; it checks the link, answers through the `trade_*` database functions as the service role (nobody else can call them), and signs the photos for an hour. Supabase does not serve web pages from functions, which is why the page itself is on Expo's hosting.
- The job's station is built into the same place by the same command: `https://punch-list.expo.app/s/#<token>` on a tablet and `https://punch-list.expo.app/tv/#<token>` on a TV, with `#sample` for the sample. Without the slash the hosting answers with the company's page, so every page starts by sending /s, /tv and /o on to the same address with the slash (found and fixed on 8 November: station links from builds 13 to 15 opened the company's page until then; they work again with nothing to update). Its calls go to the same function with the station's token as `s`: alone for the board (`station_page`), or with a company for that company's list, opened through the company's own link (`station_company` finds it, or makes it), which never leaves the function. The app gets, makes and turns off the station's link through `job_station`, so it needs a signal. `npx tsx tools/station-e2e.ts` walks it against a stand-in, after `COMPANY_PAGE_FUNCTION=http://localhost:4321/fn node tools/build-company-page.mjs`.
- Later: the page can move to the company's own address (for example `link.rossbuilt.com`, or one for Punch List) by pointing a domain at Expo's hosting and setting `EXPO_PUBLIC_COMPANY_PAGE_URL` in the app's build.

## Reading the plans (7 November 2026)

- The function: `supabase/functions/read-plans`, live since 7 November (version 2 since build 13, with the allowance; version 3 since build 14, with Read a List), deployed with verify_jwt on (the caller signs in). The phone puts the plans in the job's folder in the photos bucket (`<job>/plans/read-<id>.pdf`, or each picture whole and in pieces as `read-<id>-<sheet>-<piece>.jpg`) and calls it with where they are. It checks the caller is switched on at a company with some of this month's allowance for Claude left and can see the job, signs each file for ten minutes, gives Claude the links (a PDF as a document, pictures as images), and answers with Claude's reply. While Claude reads it sends a space every ten seconds, so the phone's connection stays open. Once Claude has answered it takes the files out of storage, as the service role, and counts what the reading cost against the company.
- `{ check: true }` answers whether the Claude key is set. The app shows Read the Plans only once it says yes, and asks again every five minutes, so setting the key needs no new build.
- `PLANS_MODEL` can name another model; it is Sonnet otherwise. To deploy: `supabase functions deploy read-plans` (or through the Supabase connector), with `index.ts`, `core.js` and `claude.ts`.
- Version 4 (live since 8 November, once Jake said to) also asks Claude which page or picture shows each level's floor plan (`sheet`), so the app keeps that sheet as the level's plan in Plans. The phone keeps it from pictures of the sheets; a page of a PDF is kept on the preview, where the page is drawn with the PDF reader, and on the iPhone a screenshot of it goes in with Add a Plan.
- Limits: a PDF up to 30 MB (Claude reads up to 32 MB and 100 pages at once), up to six pictures. On Supabase's free plan a function can run for 150 seconds, which is room for a set of plans; a very big set may need the floor plan pages sent on their own.

## Claude in the app (7 November 2026)

- The function: `supabase/functions/ai`, live since 7 November (version 1; version 2 since build 14, with Round 2's tasks), deployed with verify_jwt on. Every other use of Claude on the iPhone goes through it: Fill In From Photo, Say It, the same work written twice (as an item is written, and Find Duplicates), and standard wording. The phone names one of the tasks in `packages/core/src/claude.ts` and sends its input, with a photo or a few when the task takes them; the function builds the words from the core, so nothing else can be asked on the key. Quick questions go to Haiku, photos and Say It to Sonnet (`QUICK_MODEL` and `SHARP_MODEL` can name others).
- Both functions need the Claude key as a function secret: in the Supabase dashboard, Edge Functions, Secrets, add `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (from console.anthropic.com, API Keys). Until it is there the app hides Claude's buttons, and it asks again every five minutes, so adding the key needs no new build.
- What each answer cost is counted in `ai_usage`, by the functions as the service role, at each model's price (Sonnet 5.5: 2 dollars a million tokens in, 10 out; Haiku 5.5: 10 cents in, 50 out). Each company has a monthly allowance: 25 dollars unless `ai_limits` says otherwise (Ross Built's is 200 dollars). Once it is used, the functions ask nothing more until the first of the month, and the app says so. An admin sees this month's cost on the Me screen, under Claude. To change a company's allowance, as the app's owner: `insert into ai_limits (org, monthly_cents) values ('<company id>', 5000) on conflict (org) do update set monthly_cents = excluded.monthly_cents;`
- The words, the tasks and the prices come from `packages/core`, so the iPhone, the preview and the functions ask the same way, and the two functions share `supabase/functions/_shared/claude.ts`. `npm run build:functions` writes `core.js` and `claude.ts` into each function's folder (a function cannot reach other folders once it is deployed), and a unit test fails when they are behind.
- `DENO=/path/to/deno npm run test:functions` runs both functions on this computer against stand-ins for Supabase and Claude: who they turn away, the tasks and photos they take, the model each task goes to, the allowance, the counting, the open connection while Claude works, and for read-plans the links Claude gets and the files taken away.
- Say It writes down what is said on the iPhone itself with Apple's speech recognition (`expo-speech-recognition`, on the phone when the phone can), so it needs a new build and asks for the microphone and speech recognition the first time. The preview link has no microphone: the words are typed there.
- Round 2 asks Claude four more things through `ai`: the done check (the photo of the problem and the done photo, on Sonnet), a company from a business card or an email signature (Sonnet), what a new item waits on, and which column is which in a spreadsheet (both Haiku). Read a List sends a punch list's PDF or pages through `read-plans` with `kind: list` and the job's names as `input`; it is counted as `read-list`.
- **The owner's page** is built by the same command into `/o`: `https://punch-list.expo.app/o/#<token>`, with `#sample` for the sample. Its calls go to `company-page` with the owner's link as `o`, through the `owner_*` database functions as the service role: `owner_page`, `owner_add_item` (the function makes the item's id and puts the photo at `<job>/<item>/owner-<uuid>.jpg` first, and takes it away again when the item does not come in) and `owner_sign_off`. The app gets, makes, renames and turns off the link through `owner_link`. A company's page in Spanish: `company-page` translates the list's lines on the builder's allowance (`org_ai_allowance`, task `translate`, Haiku), each line once, and keeps them in `translations` by the line's SHA-256; it waits up to 12 seconds, then finishes in the background for the next opening. `ai` writes the owner's weekly update (`owner-update`, Sonnet) and sums up the companies' answers (`company-summary`, Haiku). Live since 8 November: the migration `owners_and_companies` (live as 20261108190856), `company-page` version 4, `ai` version 3, and the pages published again, once Jake said to.
- A company's done photo comes to `company-page` as `action: photo`, a JPEG in base64 made smaller on the phone (the one call allowed past 8,000 characters, up to 3,000,000). The function asks `trade_photo_folder` for the item's folder, puts the photo at `<job>/<item>/done-<uuid>.jpg` as the service role, then `trade_add_photo` puts it on the item (at most ten done photos on an item); if that refuses, the file is taken away again. Live since 7 November: the migration `done_photos` (live as 20261108031902), `company-page` version 3, and the companies' pages and the station published again.
- Merging two items is the database function `merge_items`: the one kept takes the other's photos and comments, what waited on the other waits on it, and the other is removed, with a line in Activity. On the phone it needs a signal and every change made on the phone up first. Not the Same in Find Duplicates is kept on each phone.

## Notifications (8 November 2026)

- The app: `expo-notifications` (in `app.json`'s plugins, which gives the iPhone build the push entitlement). Once someone turns notifications on (from Updates, or in Me), the app asks iOS once, gets the phone's Expo push token for the project (`extra.eas.projectId` in `app.json`) and keeps it on the server for whoever is signed in with `save_push_token`; turning them off or signing out takes the phone off with `drop_push_token`. Each time the app opens, the token is kept up to date. Tapping a notification opens its item, or its job. The preview page only shows the steps: it has no phone to tell.
- The database: `push_tokens` (nobody reads or writes it directly), `save_push_token` and `drop_push_token` for the app, and for the company-page function only, `push_targets` (from a company's link or the owner's link: the switched-on people on the job's team, or the company's admins when the job has nobody on its team; nobody for a link taken back or a closed job) and `forget_push_tokens`. Migration `search_words_and_notifications` (live as 20261109164927), which also keeps a photo's words.
- The sender: `company-page`, once the page has its answer, so telling the team never slows or fails the page. It tells for Done, Confirmed and a comment from a company's page (or the station), and for what the owner adds and their sign off; not for a done photo alone, the hours, Not Done or anything turned away. It posts to Expo's push service (`https://exp.host/--/api/v2/push/send`, at most 100 at once), with the job's name as the title and Activity's words as the line, and forgets a phone Expo says is no longer registered. `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN` (a function secret) is needed only if enhanced push security is turned on for the project on Expo; it is not. `EXPO_PUSH_URL` points it at a stand-in for the function checks.

## Fish from Buildertrend (9 November 2026)

A one-time, read-only import of a job's Completion List out of Buildertrend, so the job does not start empty in the app. Nothing is ever written to Buildertrend, and none of a job's own data is in this repository: the read, the hints and the list stay in `local/`.

**How the read gets out.** No pasted session. Jake's Chrome is signed in to Buildertrend, and a few lines run in a Buildertrend tab do the three reads `readForImport` does (the task field definitions, the tags, and `POST /apix/v2/Tasks/list` for the job), plus each company's record (`GET /api/Subs/<id>`) for its people. The page can hand only about 1,400 characters back at a time, so nothing comes back that way. Instead the page sends what it read to a temporary holding table in the app's own database, and says only the SHA-256 of what it sent:

```js
// In a tab on buildertrend.net. Reads only. <URL> and <KEY> are the app's Supabase address and publishable key.
const H = { accept: 'application/json, text/plain, */*', portaltype: '1' };
const get = (p) => fetch(p, { credentials: 'include', headers: H }).then((r) => r.json());
const list = await fetch('/apix/v2/Tasks/list', { method: 'POST', credentials: 'include', headers: { ...H, 'content-type': 'application/merge-patch+json' }, body: JSON.stringify({ jobIds: [JOB] }) }).then((r) => r.json());
// Every task carries every tag on the account, each with a selected flag: keep a task's own.
const tasks = list.tasks.map((t) => ({ ...t, tags: (t.tags ?? []).filter((x) => x.selected === true) }));
const bytes = new TextEncoder().encode(JSON.stringify({ readAt: new Date().toISOString(), jobId: JOB, definitions: await get('/apix/v2/CustomFields/15/definitions'), tags: await get('/apix/v2/Tasks/tags'), list: { ...list, tasks, watchers: undefined } }));
const sha256 = [...new Uint8Array(await crypto.subtle.digest('SHA-256', bytes))].map((b) => b.toString(16).padStart(2, '0')).join('');
let binary = ''; for (let i = 0; i < bytes.length; i += 32768) binary += String.fromCharCode.apply(null, bytes.subarray(i, i + 32768));
const b64 = btoa(binary);
for (let part = 0; part * 1e6 < b64.length; part++) await fetch('<URL>/rest/v1/rpc/import_mailbox_put_raw', { method: 'POST', headers: { apikey: '<KEY>', Authorization: 'Bearer <KEY>', 'content-type': 'application/json' }, body: JSON.stringify({ part, body: b64.slice(part * 1e6, (part + 1) * 1e6) }) });
sha256;
```

Fish's list was 38 megabytes as Buildertrend sends it and 2.5 with each task's own tags only.

**The mailbox.** `tools/import-mailbox.sql` makes the holding table and three functions: the page may only add a part (it is told no key), and the workspace reads a part and puts the finished list back with a key kept in `local/secrets/import.key`, of which the database knows only the hash. It is run by hand on the live project with Jake's OK, never as a migration, is not in the sync, and is removed as soon as the list is loaded. `node tools/import-mailbox.mjs get raw 0 local/fish-raw.json <sha256>` brings the read into the workspace and refuses it when the hash is not the one the page said.

**The import.** `npm run import:buildertrend -- from local/fish-raw.json subs local/fish-subs.json hints local/fish-hints.json` writes `local/fish-import.json` and says, in plain sentences, everything that did not fit. The hints are what the job's own list needs said (`ImportOptions` in `packages/buildertrend/src/importList.ts`): its room list with levels, as Job Setup takes it pasted; other names for a place; a company's name spelled properly; a title for one over 80 characters; and whether the Action Items come too. What the first real read showed, and what the import does about it:

- Every tag on the account comes with every task, marked selected or not; a dropdown with nothing picked is -1; the Location field is gone; Duration and Priority were added. Duration becomes the estimated time at 8 hours a day, and Priority 1 High becomes the app's Priority.
- Groups left from before the room standard are named for a trade (Stucco, Carpentry), each item with its place as a second tag and at the end of its title. With a room list given, such a group is not a location: its items go to the location their tag names, and the place comes off the title.
- Waiting On is a short dropdown (Subcontractor, Owner, Supplier), kept as it reads. An open item that is not ready and says nothing waits on Not Said, since the app takes no item that waits on nothing.
- Purchase or Change Order holds words as often as a number (None, Ross Built, Owner): only a number is kept.
- In the app the trade follows the company, so each item comes in under its company's own trade (the tag on most of its items), and each company is on the job once.
- Work a company marked complete itself comes in open with the day it was marked done, under To Check: only the builder completes an item. Work the builder completed comes in complete, dated by Buildertrend's last status change.
- Tasks typed Task inside a room group are items of that room. The other open ones typed Task are the builder's Action Items: they come in ready, assigned to the person they are assigned to, at the location their tag names or at Office. Their due dates do not come: the app has no place for one yet.
- A company's people are one for each email on its Buildertrend record, with its phone on the first; a company with only a phone has one person, reached by text.

**The loader.** `node tools/import-mailbox.mjs put local/fish-import.json` puts the list in the mailbox, and `tools/load-import.sql` loads it into a job that is already in the app, as a function in `pg_temp` that is gone when the session ends. It adds the job's locations after the ones it has, the trades the list uses (a starter trade the list has its own word for is renamed to that word: HVAC to Air Conditioning, Cabinets to Cabinetry, Trim Carpentry to Carpentry, Landscaping to Landscape), the companies, each on the job under its own trade, their people, and the items in walk order so their numbers follow it. Anything already there is left as it is, so a second run adds nothing. Open work is dated the day it is loaded, since what the app measures from that date (how long a company takes to confirm) starts then; done work keeps the day it went on the list in Buildertrend. The items are added by nobody, so Updates reads "Buildertrend added 760 items from a list".

**Rehearsed first.** The load was run on a fresh local database set up like the live job (the migrations, then the job with its nine starting areas), twice: the second run added nothing. Every loaded item was then checked with the app's own rules (`checkItem`), against the list field by field, and for its number following the walk order.
