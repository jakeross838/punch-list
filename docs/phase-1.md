# Phase 1: The camera walk, the list and job setup

**Goal:** the app on Jake's iPhone, working with no signal. He opens the app, picks the job, opens the camera, snaps a photo, marks it up or leaves it as is, and fills in the item as easily as possible. The list is kept by level and room, and filters and defaults are his to set.

**Plan:** [Punch List App: Version 1 Plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/8aKXo8iiTrKoH78Q1Mce7a), section 5. Photos and markup moved into this phase on 29 October 2026, at Jake's direction ("open app, select job, open camera, snap picture, mark up or leave as is, then fill out info").

**Design:** [Punch List App Design](https://claude.ai/artifact/MkYkFBRyhwxCDGG488omuC), the approved canvas. Jake's review asked for less text up front, the rest one tap in, very calm and easy to use, with a clear way to check things complete.

## The walk

1. **Jobs** opens to the job.
2. **The job** lists each level and its locations with how many items are open, how many are ready, what is past due (in red) and how long the open work takes, and a check where nothing is open. Each level's heading has its own totals, and the job's title says the whole job's. Drafts sit on their own at the top when there are any; anything past due shows as a red count on Schedule. The bar at the bottom has the camera in the middle, Schedule on the left and View on the right. Search is under the magnifying glass, Send Lists under the share button, and the plus types a new item in with no camera.
3. **The camera** shows the location the photos go to at the top; tap it to change it. The shutter, the photo library, and Skip Photo for an item without one. **Draft** (beside Item, over the shutter) is for going around fast: each photo saves as it is taken, as a draft in the location at the top, and Claude starts filling it in from the photo right away (the title, where exactly, the trade and the company). Any it could not do on the walk, with no signal say, are filled in when Drafts opens. Sitting down, each draft is checked over, marked up if needed, and Next puts it on the lists.
4. **Markup**, made for the job site: draw, arrow, circle, box, **Measure** (drag from one end to the other and type the size; it is written out on the photo as it is typed, 24 reads 24 inches and 2'6" reads 2 feet 6 inches, with Inches or Feet for a number on its own), **Number** (tap each spot with the same work to number it 1, 2, 3) and **Note** (the standard words one tap away: Touch up, Caulk, Patch, Clean, Scratch, Chip, Crack, Gap, Dent, Loose, Missing, Install here, or type one). Red, yellow, white or black, with Undo, and a line under the photo says how to use the tool picked. The marks have a professional look: fine lines with a soft edge so they show on a white wall or a dark floor, solid arrowheads, notes and sizes set in label tags, measurements drawn like a dimension on the plans, and numbered spots as pins. Claude reads the sizes and spots when it fills in an item, so several numbered spots come back as, for example, "Three spots, numbered on the photo." A photo cropped to fit (the item screen, thumbnails) moves to keep its marks in view. Next leaves the photo as it is when nothing was drawn.
5. **New Item:** the location is already filled in. Typing a title offers the standard titles, and picking one fills in the trade, the company and how long the work usually takes. **Estimated Time** is one tap: 15 minutes, 30 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours, 4 hours or 8 hours (a full day), or Other for the rest; tap the chosen one again to clear it. Ready is a checkbox: unchecking it opens a small box to tick what the item waits on, one thing or several (the job's companies, Ross Built, the owner, the design team, or something else typed in), and when it should be ready. Waiting On is always a who: a company, Ross Built, the owner, the design team, or someone else typed in; what it waits for goes in the description. The type is one tap: Deficiency (wrong or damaged, to fix), Incomplete (scope completion: their work, not done yet) or Additional (extra work that needs a price, with its purchase order or change order number once it is priced). Bought Out is not asked for; it follows the type and the number. Added fields wait under More Details. Save and Next goes straight back to the camera for the next one; Save goes back to the list. With a photo just taken, Add to an Item puts it on an item already in the location instead (one typed in earlier, say), and the camera says so; the same title already in the location offers it too. Saving without the company (or the title, with a photo) keeps it as a draft instead of stopping. The same title already in the location is pointed out. Fill In From Photo asks Claude to fill in what is blank from the photo, its marks, the location, the standard titles (with their usual times) and the companies on the job, the estimated time included; it never overwrites what was typed.
6. **A location** lists its items, each complete at a glance: the circle to check it off, the title with its estimated time at the right, the company doing it and the type of work, what it waits on and when it should be ready (with the days in red when it is past due), and the photo. Lists that are not one location's say where each item is; a company's list leaves out the company. The title says how many are open and how long they take. Drafts stay in Drafts until they are filled in, with a pencil instead of the circle. An open item with no photo has an empty photo slot: tap it and the camera takes the photo straight onto that item, marked up or not. New Item under the list types one in with no camera, and its Save and Next starts the next one right there. Checking an item off says Completed, with Undo. Completed items wait under their own heading. The bottom bar steps to the location before or after, with the camera between. Checking off a company's last item in a location offers to mark ready what was waiting on that company there.
7. **An item** is the same simple form, changed right where it is: tap the title or description to retype it, tick Ready, pick another location or company, Mark Up a photo. Each change saves as it is made; there is no Edit button. Its photos with their marks are above (tap for the whole photo, Mark Up or Add Photo), its visit is below, and Mark Complete is the big button. A draft says what it still needs, or that Claude is filling it in, and steps to the next one; Next puts a draft that has everything on the lists. A draft someone is typing in is left to them.
8. **View and Filter** groups the job by location, trade, company or what items wait on (Ready and Needs a Price first, then each thing waited on with how many items it holds up, the late ones first, so work that can go together shows as a batch), and narrows it to exactly the work in question. Every filter takes several values, one tap each: Status (Ready, Not Ready, Past Due, Needs a Price), Type, Company, Trade, Level, Waiting On, Added By, Estimated Time (estimated or not yet), Visit (scheduled or not) and Photo (has one or not). Within a filter any value matches; across filters, every one must, so Ready, SMS Carpentry and Triple H Painting is the two companies' ready work. The sheet says how many open items match as you go. The filters that are on show as chips under the title of the job and of every list in it: tap a chip to take it off, or Clear All. Save as My Default makes every job open that way. A Waiting On list moves the Ready By for all its items at once, or with Stop Waiting marks them all ready (with Undo).
9. **Item Form** turns the form's fields on or off (Estimated Time among them), sets what a new item starts as, adds Ross Built's own fields (text, a list, yes or no, or a date), and keeps the standard title list, each title with how long the work usually takes.
10. **Past Due**, the first thing on the Schedule, lists everything late, the latest first, with the days: not ready past its Ready By, or still open after its visit ended.
11. **Schedule** is built from the list: what is past due, each company's visits this week, next week and later (confirmed, or our best guess), and the companies with work not scheduled yet. Each company's **plan** shows its visits, what is ready now and not scheduled, what comes free and when, what has no date yet, and additional work that needs a price. Every batch says how long it takes. Ready work longer than a day splits into full working days of 8 hours, location by location (a small item later in the house can finish a day), so Schedule Day 1 books one day of work and Schedule All books all of it. Schedule picks the start (with more than a day of notice, and a day of handoff after what it waits on is done), the working days, whether the company confirmed it, and which items it covers. The visit totals the items picked and says when they need more days or fewer (Make It 2 Days), and Fill the Day adds the company's small ready work, in floor order, until the days are full. A visit with more work than its days says so in red. Ready means priced too: additional work is scheduled once it has its order number.
12. **Send Lists** (the share button on the job) lists every company with open work, how much of it is ready now, and what is new since its last list. Tap one for its list as a PDF in the house document style: its scheduled visits with their items, what is ready now, then what is waiting grouped by what it waits on (soonest first), each batch with a line for the days it takes, then additional work that needs a price, and blank lines to add anything missing; the location on every line, each item's first photo with its markup, New beside anything not on the last list, and page numbers in the footer. It opens in the phone's PDF viewer to look over and send by email or text; the share button beside each company goes straight to sending, and Send All goes through every company in one pass. Each list sent is recorded, with Undo. The owner and the design team get what Ross Built needs from them and by when. The whole job list, level by level and location by location with the trade, company and any days past due on every line, is for Ross Built; it leaves the photos off to stay short. **Make a List**, at the top, makes any other list. It starts from what the job shows now, so most lists are Make a List, then Preview: the filters already on show as chips, each one off with a tap, and Change the Filters opens every filter, single locations included. It groups by location (level by level), trade, company or what it waits on, with Show Completed; Each Line Shows picks the details on every line (description, location, company, trade, type, ready or waiting, estimated time, photo); and it can add blank lines and a line for how many days each group takes. The page says which filters made it, totals each group's time, and suggests its title from the filters (the company, trade, location or level), which can be typed over.

**Job Setup** (on the job's menu) has the job's name and address at the top, changed right where they are, then three tabs. **Locations** come from pasting the room list from the plans; tap one to rename it, or remove it from the job once it has no open items (with Undo; pasting it again brings it back). **Companies** are added with the trade they do on this job; tap one to rename it (on every job, and anything waiting on it keeps waiting on it), change its trade on this job (its items move with it), or remove it from the job once it has no open items there (with Undo). **Waiting On** adds the job's own extra choices.

**Activity** (on the job's menu) shows who added, changed, completed, photographed, scheduled or sent what on the job, newest first, a day at a time: "Sam Rivera completed Grout shower floor", "Jake Ross scheduled Triple H Painting". Several changes one person makes to one item within a few minutes read as one line, and the items put on a visit count on the visit's line. Tap a line about an item to open it. Each item's **History**, at the bottom of the item, shows the same for that item. The database keeps the log, stamped with whoever was signed in, so it is the same on every phone and nobody can change it.

## What gets built

| Piece | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone app | `apps/mobile` | Expo SDK 58, TypeScript, Expo Router. The walk above, job setup, and the item rules. The camera is `expo-camera`, the photo library `expo-image-picker`, markup is drawn with `react-native-svg`, and photos are kept in the app's own folder until they upload. The lists become PDFs with `expo-print` (the house font, Nunito Sans, and the photos are written into the page, and `pdf-lib` adds the footer with page numbers) and go out through the share sheet. Fill In From Photo calls Claude through a server function on the phone, and through the preview page's own Claude access on the preview. Built to look and work like Apple's own apps (see Look and feel). |
| Core rules | `packages/core` | The data types and the rules the app enforces: not ready needs Waiting On, one company and one trade per item, only Ross Built completes, removing never deletes, room labels and floor order from a pasted list. Also the marks (points on the photo, drawn at any size), title suggestions and what a title fills in, the form and view settings, the added fields, and the lists that go out (each company's, and the whole job) with their printed pages. Shared by the app, the server and the tests. |
| Database | `supabase/` | Migrations for the schema and access rules, the private photos bucket, plus a seed for a test job of 600 generated items. |
| Sync | `powersync/` | The PowerSync service config and sync streams: every signed-in Ross Built user gets every job; each person gets only their own settings. |

## Look and feel

The app follows Apple's design for iPhone, modeled on Camera, Markup, Reminders and Settings. The parts live in `apps/mobile/src/ui`; every screen is built from them.

- **Theme:** white and light gray, near black text, and one accent, Ross Built slate blue, for everything you can tap. Red only for removing and past due. Status is said in words, with a small gray icon where it helps.
- **Crisp lists:** text one step under the iPhone's default size (the Small setting), tight rows, thin hairlines and tighter corners, so a screen holds more of the list and every row says more. A location's row has its count, what is ready, what is past due and its time; an item's row has its time, its company and type, and what it waits on. Everything else is one tap in.
- **The camera bar:** a large camera button in the middle of the bottom bar on every job and room screen, big enough to hit without looking.
- **Navigation and sheets:** large titles that shrink into the bar, New Item, Send Lists, View and Item Form as sheets with Cancel and Done, the camera full screen, and Cancel asks before throwing away a new item.
- **Motion and touch:** rows light up the instant they are touched; buttons press in; the circle fills and the check pops in.

On the iPhone these are the system's own: the native navigation bar, SF Symbols, native menus and action sheets, page sheets that close with a swipe down, the live camera, the system switch and date picker, swipe back, and haptic taps. The preview page draws the same design in the browser; there the shutter opens the iPhone's own camera through the browser, and the live viewfinder, haptics and swipe back can only be judged on the TestFlight build.

## Data model, Phase 1 slice

| Table | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| people | Ross Built users by email, with active on or off. Only active people can read or write anything. |
| jobs | Name and address. |
| locations | Each location on a job (a room or area from the plans, or one of the shared ones): name, plan number, label, level, floor order. |
| trades | One standard list. |
| companies | Name, usual trade, phone, email. |
| job_companies | Which companies are on a job, for which trade. |
| job_waiting_on | Extra Waiting On choices per job (Ross Built, Owner, the design team by name). The job's companies are choices automatically. |
| items | The Completion List item: location, number, title, description, trade, company, estimated time in hours, type (deficiency, completion or added_scope, shown as Deficiency, Incomplete and Additional), Ready, Waiting On (one thing or several), Ready By, the order number, Bought Out (kept by the database from the type and the order number, for Buildertrend), status, who completed it and when, source, the answers to the added fields, unfinished (a draft), its visit, an empty Buildertrend link. A draft may wait for its title, company and trade, and cannot be completed. |
| visits | A company's trip to the job for a batch of items: first day, working days, confirmed or our best guess. An item goes only on its own company's visit. Never deleted; removing sets removed. |
| list_sends | Each list that went out: which list, the items on it, when and by whom, so the next one marks what is new. Undo sets removed. |
| item_history | Every change to an item: who, when, field, before, after, source. Written by the database, not the app. |
| job_activity | Everything else that happens on a job: photos added, marked up or removed, visits scheduled, changed or removed, lists sent or taken back, and setup (locations, companies, the job's name and address). Who, when, what kind, and what it was about. Written by the database, never changed or deleted. |
| title_library | Standard titles by trade, how often used, approved or not, and how long the work usually takes. Picking one fills in the trade, the company and the estimated time. |
| photos | An item's photos: size, order, where it is in storage once uploaded, and the marks drawn on it. Never deleted; removing sets removed. The picture itself is in the private photos bucket. |
| custom_fields | The fields Ross Built adds to the item form: name, kind (text, list, yes or no, date) and the choices for a list. |
| person_settings | Each person's Item Form setup and the view they saved as their default. A person sees only their own. |

Item numbers are job-wide and assigned by the database on first sync; an item made offline shows "New" until then.

## Sign-in

A one-time code sent to the person's email. Anyone can request a code, but an account whose email is not an active person sees nothing: the access rules check the email on every read and write.
