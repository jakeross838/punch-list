## Setting up a job's locations: read the plans, or type them in (7 November 2026)

Jake: "There has to be a better way to setup the jobs, location specifically. Maybe you can manually add and edit or upload the plans to get as much info out as possible?"

- **Read the Plans,** in Job Setup's Locations and on a new job's first screen. Choose the plan PDF from Files, take pictures of printed sheets, or choose pictures or screenshots of them. Claude reads the floor plans for each level's rooms and their numbers, in the order to walk them (each closet and bath right after the room it opens off), the areas outside the house (Pool and Driveway unless a site plan shows none, and anything else outside, like a Dock), and the job's name and address from the title block. A whole set of plans is fine: Claude finds the floor plans in it.
- **Checked before anything is added:** everything Claude found, by level. Tap one to leave it out, or the pencil to fix its name. The title block's name and address are there to check too; the address is checked already when the job has none. A room typed in on a walk with no number gets its number and level from the plans (Mudroom becomes Mudroom 120 on Level 1), and one already on the job stays as it is. A name the plans repeat with no number is told apart by its level (Level 2 Closet). Add puts them on in the plans' walk order, among the job's own.
- **Typing them in:** each level ends in an Add row. Type "Kitchen 109" and Return adds it and stays there for the next. Add a Level starts a new one. A level's ••• renames it or moves it up or down, and a location's sheet has Level now, to move it to another level (to the end of it). A job with no locations starts with Level 1 to type into, and Add the Areas Outside puts the four elevations, the roof, pool, driveway, site and whole house on. Paste a List and Reorder are still there.
- **The walk order** keeps each level together and the outside and whole house areas last everywhere: Job Setup, the job, the walk, the pickers, the filters and the templates. A level added later goes before the outside areas.
- **On the iPhone** the plans go up to the job's folder in storage, the new read-plans function gives Claude a link to each, and they are taken away once read; pictures go whole and cut into overlapping pieces Claude reads at full size. Read the Plans shows once the function is deployed and has the Claude key (`setup.md`, Reading the plans). **On the preview** it uses the viewer's own Claude: a PDF is read on the page into the text on each page with where each piece sits, and pictures go as pictures.
- No database change: an admin could already change a location's level and place. The live writes checks cover moving one, renaming a level, putting one level above another and adding what the plans found. Jake said to put the function live: read-plans is on the live project (version 1), and it turns away anyone not signed in at a company. It shows on the iPhone once the Claude key is set.

## The job's station, for a tablet or a TV in the house (7 November 2026)

Jake: "a feature would be to be able to broadcast these lists and such on TV or tablet in a central station of a house for subs to click on their assignments and work through the lists."

- **Turn it on from the job:** the job's menu has Job Station. Turn On the Station makes the job's link, with a code to scan with the tablet's camera, Copy the Link and Open the Station, and the same for the TV board. Make a New Link stops the old one; Turn Off the Station stops it altogether. Anyone who can see the job can do this; it needs a signal.
- **On the tablet:** every company with work on the job, the one with the most ready work first. Each tile says how much is ready, waiting, to be quoted and to check, and what to do next, in the same order as the company's own list. A company taps its name for its list: numbered in the order to do it, with Confirm, how long it takes, Mark Done and comments, the same as its own page. The name typed is kept on the station for that company. ‹ All Companies goes back, and after three minutes untouched the station goes back by itself for whoever comes next.
- **On a TV:** the same board in large type on a dark screen, six companies at a time, moving to the next six every 15 seconds: each company's ready, waiting and to check, and its next three items, numbered. A code on the screen opens the station on a phone. It keeps itself up to date every minute and needs no one to touch it. It stands on its end too.
- **Safe to leave up:** the station opens a company's list through that company's own link, so everything the company's page checks still holds, and the screen never sees a company's link. Anyone with the station's link sees every company's list on the job and can answer for any of them, which is why the app says to keep it to the house; marked done still waits for your team to check it.
- **The database:** `job_stations` (one working link per job, never read directly), `job_station` for the app, and `station_page` and `station_company` for the company-page function alone (`job_station` migration). A deleted job takes its station with it. Jake said to put it live: the migration is on the live project (as 20261107181556), the company-page function answers the station (version 2), and the station is on Expo's hosting at /s and /tv.
- Checked: the database checks (the link made, made again, replaced and turned off; who can call what; the board's work; a company's list through its own link; a replaced link opening nothing, and never a company page), the station walked in Chromium on a tablet and a TV against a stand-in (the order, a company's list and back, Mark Done under the name kept for that company, going back after three minutes, the TV's pages and code, a turned off station, the sample), the company's page again after it was split to share its list with the station, and the full preview walk-through (58 steps, with the Job Station sheet). ## Markup that looks professional, and one Assigned To (7 November 2026)

Jake, on build 8: "Can we really fine tune the markup feature, just seems kinda cheesy. The most common uses are circles, arrows and arrows with text." And: "remove the company thing, should just be assigned to, kinda the same thing. Again, simple and effective."

- **The marks:** a plainer, sharper look, like a drawing marked up by hand in red: solid lines with round ends, a sharp notched arrowhead, words in a square label in the mark's color, and a thin faint halo that keeps a mark clear on a white wall or a dark floor without reading as an outline. A note near the edge of the photo stays on it. The same look on the phone, the printed lists and the company's page.
- **The tools:** Circle, Arrow, Note and Draw up front, labeled; Box, Measure and Number under More. Note does arrows with text: drag from where the words go to the spot, then tap one of the standard words (one row now) or type. A tap places words alone. The tool, color and unit picked last carry over to the next photo.
- **Changing a mark:** tap it to pick it. Drag it to move it; drag a square to change its shape (the ends of an arrow, two corners of a circle or a box, the tip of a note's arrow; a note's words move on their own and the arrow keeps pointing). Tap a color to change it, Edit Words or Edit Size, or Delete (numbered spots after it move down). A second tap on a note opens its words.
- **Undo and Redo** at the top, like Apple's Markup, back and forth through every change. Retake and Cancel ask first when there are marks that would be lost.
- **Assigned To:** one field in place of Company and Assigned To. Pick a company (on this job, or any other) or someone on your team. A company gets the item on its list, its page and its emails, numbered in order as before; someone on the team gets it on their My Items. Picking one clears the other. A new task added from Not Ready is assigned the same way, and Select's Assign takes a company or a person for all the items picked, with Undo.
- Everywhere it shows: the item's summary and form, its row, View and Filter (By Location, Assigned To, Waiting On; one Assigned To filter with the companies and the team), Make a List, the printed lists, the spreadsheet (one Assigned To column), what work waits on ("Waiting on Taylor Brooks"), and Activity ("assigned Install pendant lights to Taylor Brooks", with nothing about the company it came off). A draft says it "Needs someone assigned".
- **The database:** an open item needs a company or someone on the team (`items_open_is_assigned`, in `assigned_to`); a draft can wait for it, and completed or removed work keeps its record. When someone deletes their account, open work that was theirs alone goes back to Drafts to be assigned. Jake said to put it in, and it went onto the live project (as 20261107175101) before build 9: the new rule is in place, the old one is gone, only someone signed in can delete their own account, and no open item is assigned to no one.
- Checked: 260 unit tests (new ones for the markup changes, Undo and Redo, and Assigned To), the type checks, lint, the database checks (with work assigned to a person alone, and a deleted account's work going to Drafts), 108 of the phone's writes, the full preview walk-through (57 steps, with the markup's new tools, picking and changing marks, Redo, and assigning to a person), and the company's page with the new mark look, which is live on Expo's hosting. ## Behaving like an iPhone app, and each company's work in order (6 November 2026)

Jake, on build 6: "check everything and if you notice it doesn't behave as an apple application would behave, fix it now." And: "set priorities for each company, like numbered do this then this then this so they can always be working on the thing that holds up the most amount of work first when possible."

- **Sheets:** a sheet with nothing changed follows the finger down and closes, as the iPhone's own do. With changes not saved yet, swiping it down, Cancel, or tapping outside it on the preview asks first: Discard Changes, or Keep Editing. New Job, New Item and every sheet with a form do this. Saving, or going to the camera for the item's photo, does not ask.
- **One sheet after another:** iOS will not show a sheet, a menu or a question while the last one is still sliding away, so a button could seem to do nothing. A menu choice now runs once the menu has gone, and a picker that leads to another (a new company, then its trade) waits for the first to close, instead of guessing at a fixed wait.
- **Typing:** Next moves to the next field (a job's name to its address; a person's name, email and phone), and Return on a one field sheet saves it. One line fields have the clear button. Sheets keep the field being typed in above the keyboard, and a drag down puts the keyboard away. Leaving a screen with a field still being typed in (an item's description, a job's name, a person, a company, a template) keeps what was typed.
- **Photos:** full screen like Photos: pinch or double tap to zoom in, drag around a zoomed photo, swipe sideways to the item's other photos, swipe down to put it away, tap to show or hide the bar. Zoomed photos stay sharp.
- **Camera:** pinch to zoom, with 1× to go back.
- **Small things:** a tap on the status bar scrolls back to the top on the job (a sideways row of chips stopped it). Larger Text grows the app's words, up to where the bars and buttons still fit them.
- **Each company's work in order:** a company's list is numbered 1, 2, 3 in the order to do it. What is ready comes first: an item marked Priority, then whatever holds up the most other work, counting every open item that waits on it down the chain, whoever does it, then the most hours of it. What is waiting comes after, soonest ready first. Each item says what it holds up ("Holds up 3 items"). The numbers are the same in the app (the company's list), on the printed list (a new Order column), on the company's page, and the email says the list is numbered. Work still to be quoted, and work the company already marked done, has no number.
- **The database:** the company's page answer says what each item holds up (`holds_up`, in `company_page`). Jake said to put the changes in, and both migrations went onto the live project the same afternoon (`walk_and_delete_job` as 20261106192919, `company_page` as 20261106192951): Delete the Job, the trades for adding a company (Ross Built has the 19 standard ones now) and the company's page answers work. ## The simple app, for any company (6 November 2026)

Jake's direction: "Remove the schedule feature for now, this is an app that allows anyone to walk into a job and take pictures of what's left to do or deficiencies or such and be able to keep a running list with marked up pictures. That's it for now, very simple. Needs to be fully capable of having companies onboard their people to use it." Ready or not and comments stay; supers and PMs use it to get lists to subcontractors so they know what work is left.

- **Out for now:** the Schedule tab and everything under it (visits, Past Due, Ready By dates, estimated time, the Schedule Email and the Three Week Look Ahead), plans with pins, the Updates bell, To Check and the company pages reached through a link. The bottom bar is Jobs, Capture and Send. The printed lists have no dates, no time, no lines for the days and no link. Their tables stay in the database, so nothing already kept is lost.
- **Stays:** jobs and locations, the camera walk, photos with markup, the running list, Ready and Waiting On, comments (internal notes stay inside the company), checklists and priority, and each company's list to send, the owner's and the whole job, the spreadsheet and Make a List.
- **Sign up:** anyone signs in with their email and a six digit code. Someone new names their company and is its admin, then adds their people under People and sends each an invite; someone an admin added goes straight in. The company's name is at the top of every list and signs the emails; an admin changes it in Me. A new company starts with the usual trades.
- **Each company sees only its own:** the database checks every read and write against the signed-in person's company and fills in the company on every row itself; each phone gets only its own company's rows; names are unique within a company. Checked with two companies side by side (`npm run test:db`).
- **Delete My Account,** in Me: the sign in goes for good, and the person's name, email and phone come off their records, which stay with the company under Deleted Account. An admin with people still at the company makes someone else an admin first. The last one at a company takes the whole company with them: its jobs, items, photos and lists.
- **Before others can sign up:** the sign in emails need the project's own sender (custom SMTP), since Supabase's own only reaches the project's team.

## The walk

1. **Jobs** opens to the job.
2. **The job** lists each level and its locations with how many items are open, how many are ready, what is past due (in red) and how long the open work takes, and a check where nothing is open. Each level's heading has its own totals, and the job's title says the whole job's. Drafts sit on their own at the top when there are any, and under them the walk going on: how many locations are walked and the next one, a tap from the camera (once every one is walked, a tap starts a new walk; Start a New Walk is also on the job's menu). Each location says when its open work is done ("Done by November 4") once all of it has a date. Anything past due shows as a red count on Schedule. **Filter** is at the top, under the title, with how the job is grouped beside it (By Location) and the filters that are on as chips after them. The bar at the bottom has the camera in the middle, Schedule on the left and Send Lists on the right. Search is under the magnifying glass, and the plus types a new item in with no camera.
3. **The camera** shows the location the photos go to at the top; tap it to change it, or **Next** when the room is done: it is marked walked, and the camera goes on to the next location in floor order ("Bathroom 116 walked. Now in Laundry 119"). The shutter never asks for anything first: with no location picked yet, the photo saves anyway, and its location is picked when it is finished. There is one way to use it: each photo saves straight away as an item in that location, a draft until someone checks it, and Claude starts filling it in from the photo right away (the title, where exactly and the company). The last one shows by the shutter: tap it to finish it now (mark it up, fix the title), and Done goes back to the camera. **Type an Item** makes one without a photo. **Common Tasks** lists the usual work for that kind of room, worked out from the location's name (in a bathroom: Trim out toilet, Install faucet, Install shower trim, towel bars, the mirror), each with the company it goes to and its usual time; tick the ones that apply and Add puts them on the list, and anything already on the list there shows as added. Under the shutter: how many locations are walked, and when everything is done, counting the walk's drafts as Claude fills them in, with how far the walk moved it ("Done by Friday, November 13, was November 10"). Any draft Claude could not do on the walk, with no signal say, is filled in when Drafts opens. Sitting down, each draft is checked over, marked up if needed, and Next puts it on the lists. A draft of something already on the list there says so, and its photo goes on that item instead with one tap (or Add to an Item, for one with another title); the draft comes off the list and stays in the history. Add Photo on an item takes the photo straight onto that item, marked up or not, and from a walk the walk carries on after it.
4. **Markup**, made for the job site: draw, arrow, circle, box, **Measure** (drag from one end to the other and type the size; it is written out on the photo as it is typed, 24 reads 24 inches and 2'6" reads 2 feet 6 inches, with Inches or Feet for a number on its own), **Number** (tap each spot with the same work to number it 1, 2, 3) and **Note** (the standard words one tap away: Touch up, Caulk, Patch, Clean, Scratch, Chip, Crack, Gap, Dent, Loose, Missing, Install here, or type one). Red, yellow, white or black, with Undo, and a line under the photo says how to use the tool picked. The marks have a professional look: fine lines with a soft edge so they show on a white wall or a dark floor, solid arrowheads, notes and sizes set in label tags, measurements drawn like a dimension on the plans, and numbered spots as pins. Claude reads the sizes and spots when it fills in an item, so several numbered spots come back as, for example, "Three spots, numbered on the photo." A photo cropped to fit (the item screen, thumbnails) moves to keep its marks in view. Next leaves the photo as it is when nothing was drawn.
5. **New Item:** kept short, the same as an item: the photo, the title, the location (already filled in), the company and Ready up front, and the rest under **Details**, with a line saying what is in it ("Incomplete · 30 minutes"). There is no trade to pick: the trade follows the company, the one it does on the job in Job Setup. Typing a title offers the standard titles, each with the company it goes to, and picking one fills in the company and how long the work usually takes. **Estimated Time** (under Details) is one tap: 15 minutes, 30 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours, 4 hours or 8 hours (a full day), or Other for the rest; tap the chosen one again to clear it. Ready is a checkbox: unchecking it opens **Not Ready** to pick what has to be done first, usually one task on the list. The location's tasks are listed first, the search finds any task on the job, and **Add a Task** puts a new one on the list right there (a mirror waits on Hang wallpaper, which was not on the list yet). For what is not a task on the list (the owner picking a fixture, the design team, Ross Built, or someone else typed in) it waits on who. Ready By is optional: without it, the item is ready by the day the tasks it waits on are scheduled to be done. Nothing can end up waiting on itself, even by way of other items. The type is one tap: Deficiency (wrong or damaged, to fix), Incomplete (scope completion: their work, not done yet) or Additional (extra work, to be quoted until it has its **order**: tick Purchase Order, Variance Purchase Order or Change Order and type its number, said in full everywhere, "Additional, Change Order 14"). Bought Out is not asked for; it follows the type and the number. Added fields are under Details too. Save and Next goes straight back to the camera for the next one; Save goes back to the list. With a photo taken for it, the same title already in the location offers to put the photo on that item instead. Saving without the company (or the title, with a photo) keeps it as a draft instead of stopping. The same title already in the location is pointed out. Fill In From Photo asks Claude to fill in what is blank from the photo, its marks, the location, the standard titles (with their usual times) and the companies on the job, the estimated time included; it never overwrites what was typed.
6. **A location** lists its items, each complete at a glance: the circle to check it off, the title with its estimated time at the right, the company doing it and the type of work, how many comments it has, what it waits on and when it should be ready (with the days in red when it is past due), and the photo. Lists that are not one location's say where each item is; a company's list leaves out the company. The title says how many are open and how long they take. Drafts stay in Drafts until they are filled in, with a pencil instead of the circle. An open item with no photo has an empty photo slot: tap it and the camera takes the photo straight onto that item, marked up or not. New Item under the list types one in with no camera, and its Save and Next starts the next one right there. Checking an item off says Completed, with Undo. Completed items wait under their own heading. The bottom bar steps to the location before or after, with the camera between. Checking off a task makes ready what waited on it, with Undo for both ("Completed Install pocket door hardware. Paint pocket doors is ready now."); something that also waits on the owner keeps waiting on the owner.
7. **An item** is the same simple form, changed right where it is: tap the title or description to retype it, tick Ready, pick another location or company, Mark Up a photo. Each change saves as it is made; there is no Edit button. Its photos with their marks are above (tap for the whole photo, Mark Up or Add Photo), then the title, the location, the company and Ready. **Details** holds the estimated time, the type, the order, the added fields and the visit, with a line saying what is in it ("Incomplete · 2 hours · Visit November 3"). **Comments** and **History** are one tap in, each row showing the latest. Mark Complete is the big button. Comments are a back and forth with the company doing the work, who said what and when; the company's own read "Mike at SMS Carpentry". **Ross Built Only** keeps a note inside Ross Built: it never shows on the company's page or prints. Tap your own comment to take it back (with Undo). A draft says what it still needs, or that Claude is filling it in, and steps to the next one; Next puts a draft that has everything on the lists. A draft someone is typing in is left to them.
8. **Filter** (at the top of the job) groups the job by location, company or what items wait on (Ready and To Be Quoted first, then each thing waited on with how many items it holds up, a task counting as the company doing it, the late ones first, so work that can go together shows as a batch), and narrows it to exactly the work in question. Every filter takes several values, one tap each: Status (Ready, Not Ready, Past Due, To Be Quoted), Type, Company, Level, Waiting On, Added By, Estimated Time (estimated or not yet), Visit (scheduled or not) and Photo (has one or not). Within a filter any value matches; across filters, every one must, so Ready, SMS Carpentry and Triple H Painting is the two companies' ready work. The sheet says how many open items match as you go. The filters that are on show as chips under the title of the job and of every list in it: tap a chip to take it off, or Clear All. Save as My Default makes every job open that way. A Waiting On list moves the Ready By for all its items at once, or with Stop Waiting marks them all ready (with Undo).
9. **Item Form** turns the form's fields on or off (Estimated Time among them), sets what a new item starts as, adds Ross Built's own fields (text, a list, yes or no, or a date), keeps the standard title list, each title with how long the work usually takes, and sets the **Common Tasks** for each kind of room (bathrooms, kitchens, laundry rooms, bedrooms, closets and pantries, living areas and halls, garages, outside) by ticking standard titles.
10. **Past Due**, the first thing on the Schedule, lists everything late, the latest first, with the days: not ready past its Ready By, or still open after its visit ended.
11. **Schedule** is a chart built from the list as it is right now: a row for each company in the order its work starts, a bar for each visit across the working days of the next three weeks and on, and the day everything with a date is done ("Everything with a date is done by Friday, November 13"). The visits that set that date, the last ones and the work they wait on, are outlined, and said under the chart, with what they wait on until when. Holidays are shaded and never get work: New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and the day after, and Christmas Day, each taken the Friday before or the Monday after when it falls on a weekend; a visit booked on one keeps its day, and its days count on past it. A visit a company asked to move from its page has a red dot, and Asked for Other Dates at the top lists them: Move It puts the visit on the dates asked for, confirmed, and Keep These Dates leaves it. Drafts are not on the schedule until they are checked; a row says how many. Visits on the schedule stay where they are: green once the company confirms, blue as our best guess. Everything else that is ready, or has a date it will be ready, gets a pale best guess that moves as the list changes: as soon as it can go, with more than a day's notice and a day of handoff after the work it waits on, in full days of 8 hours, location by location, never on a day the company already has a visit. Work that waits on a task follows that task however far back the chain goes, and work left open on a visit that is over gets a new best guess. **Save the Best Guess** keeps those dates, with Undo. Tap a bar for its visit, or a company for its plan. What has no date yet (waiting on someone with no Ready By, or past its Ready By) and the additional work to be quoted are listed under the chart. Each company's **plan** shows its visits, what is ready now and not scheduled, what comes free and when, what has no date yet, and additional work to be quoted. Every batch says how long it takes. Ready work longer than a day splits into full working days of 8 hours, location by location (a small item later in the house can finish a day), so Schedule Day 1 books one day of work and Schedule All books all of it. Schedule picks the start (with more than a day of notice, and a day of handoff after what it waits on is done), the working days, whether the company confirmed it, and which items it covers. The visit totals the items picked and says when they need more days or fewer (Make It 2 Days), and Fill the Day adds the company's small ready work, in floor order, until the days are full. A visit with more work than its days says so in red. Ready means priced too: additional work is scheduled once it has its purchase order, variance purchase order or change order. Work that waits on another task comes free when that task's visit is done, and is booked the working day after; while that task has no visit, the plan offers to schedule its company. The plan also opens the company's page, the way the company sees it, and has the **comments on its whole list** ("We can do all the trim work Monday and Tuesday next week").
12. **Send Lists** (at the bottom right of the job) starts with the **Three Week Look Ahead**, the schedule for everyone: each company's visits for the next three weeks (from next Monday once it is Friday), green when confirmed and blue for our best guess, the visits that set the finish date outlined and named, the holidays shaded and said, then what each visit covers with the location on every line (and any other dates the company asked for), and what has no date yet. Then it lists every company with open work, how much of it is ready now, and what is new since its last list. Tap one for its list as a PDF in the house document style: its scheduled visits with their items, what is ready now, then what is waiting grouped by what it waits on (the task, who is doing it and where; soonest first), each batch with a line for the days it takes, then the additional work **To Be Quoted**, with a line for the company's price on each item, and blank lines to add anything missing; the location on every line, each item's first photo with its markup, New beside anything not on the last list, and page numbers in the footer. It opens in the phone's PDF viewer to look over and send by email or text; the share button beside each company goes straight to sending, and Send All goes through every company in one pass. Each list sent is recorded, with Undo. The owner and the design team get what Ross Built needs from them and by when. The whole job list, level by level and location by location with the company, type, what it waits on (with any days past due), its estimated time and its photo on every line, and each location's time, is for Ross Built. Include Photos puts each item's first photo, with its markup, on every list. A company's list prints the comments on its whole list under the heading and each item's comments under the item; the whole job list prints each item's comments too. The owner's and the design team's lists leave them off, and Ross Built only notes never print. **Make a List**, at the top, makes any other list. It starts from what the job shows now, so most lists are Make a List, then Preview: the filters already on show as chips, each one off with a tap, and Change the Filters opens every filter, single locations included. It groups by location (level by level), company or what it waits on, with Show Completed; Each Line Shows picks the details on every line (description, location, company, type, ready or waiting, estimated time, comments, photo); and it can add blank lines and a line for how many days each group takes. The page says which filters made it, totals each group's time, and suggests its title from the filters (the company, location or level), which can be typed over.

**The company's page.** Each company gets a private page for each job, opened from the link on its list, with no account to set up. It has the company's scheduled visits, each with **These Dates Work** (the visit is confirmed and turns green on the schedule) or **Other Dates** (the day it can start and how many days, for Ross Built to move it), and its open work (ready now, waiting, to be quoted), each item with its photo, where it is and what it waits on. The company types its name once and comments on any item or on its whole list; what it writes shows in the app and in Activity right away, and prints on its next list. Ross Built only notes never show there. A link can be turned off, and then the page shows nothing. On the preview, the company's plan opens the page as the company sees it; the page on the web, from the link on the list, comes with the live database.

**Job Setup** (on the job's menu) has the job's name and address at the top, changed right where they are, then three tabs. **Locations** come from pasting the room list from the plans; tap one to rename it, or remove it from the job once it has no open items (with Undo; pasting it again brings it back). **Companies** are added with the trade they do on this job (an item's trade follows its company, so the item never asks for it); tap one to rename it (on every job, and anything waiting on it keeps waiting on it), change its trade on this job (its items move with it), or remove it from the job once it has no open items there (with Undo). **Waiting On** adds the job's own extra choices.

**Activity** (on the job's menu) shows who added, changed, completed, photographed, scheduled, sent or commented on what on the job, newest first, a day at a time: "Sam Rivera completed Grout shower floor", "Jake Ross scheduled Triple H Painting", "Mike at SMS Carpentry commented on Adjust cabinet door", "Mike at SMS Carpentry asked to move its visit". Each item's **History**, one tap in on the item, shows the same for that item. ## Sign-in

A six digit code sent to the person's email: no password. Only people Ross Built has added under People and not switched off get one: the database's sign in check (`hook_only_ross_built_people`, set as Supabase Auth's Before User Created hook) turns anyone else away before an account is made, with the sentence the app shows. Even so, an account whose email is not an active person sees nothing: the access rules check the email on every read and write. Account, from the gear on Jobs, says who you are signed in as, your role, what you see and your jobs, with Sign Out. Signing out clears what is on the phone; it all comes back when you sign in again.

## Copy and paste, drag, and templates (2 November 2026)

- Select on any list, or a long press on an item, picks items. The bar at the bottom then does it to all of them at once: Move (to another location), Copy, Assign, Complete, and Template (save them as a template, for admins). Each comes with Undo.

3. **Owner sign off:** the owner signs off the job or a location on their page with their name; it shows in Activity.
4. **Claude drafts the owner's weekly update** from what moved this week: positive, no one singled out, ending with a happy weekend, with photos of the work done, to copy or share.
5. **A company's page in Spanish:** a company set to Spanish sees its list and the comments translated by Claude, with the original a tap away.
6. **Send by Text:** a company's link and a short note by text message, next to email.
7. **One link for all of a company's jobs:** a company's page lists the other jobs it has open work on with the builder.
8. **Company report card:** on a company's screen, what is open, what it finished in the last 30 days, its average days to finish, what is past due, and when it last opened its list.
9. **Claude sums up the companies' answers:** what they confirmed, marked done and asked this week, in a few lines on Activity.
10. **Nudge:** one tap drafts a reminder to each company with items it has not confirmed, or a list it has not opened in a week.

**Round 4: finding things and the day**

1. **Search everything:** items, locations, companies, comments and people across all jobs.
2. **Ask Claude to find:** "what is ready for Gator on Level 2" becomes the list's filters.
3. **Search photos by what is in them:** Claude describes each photo in a few words once, so search finds a water stain or an outlet cover.
4. **Today:** one screen across jobs with my items, To Check, the companies' new answers and the ready work.
5. **The day's walk:** the day's items in walking order, location by location.
6. **End of day note:** Claude writes what got done and what was found today, to copy into the daily log.
7. **Recent and starred:** the items opened last, and a star to keep an item at the top.
8. **Saved views:** a set of filters and grouping with a name (Ready for Paint), one tap away.
9. **Updates:** what changed since I last looked, across jobs.
10. **Notifications:** when a company marks work done, confirms or comments.
